1. Conduct a permutation test

Conduct a permutation test to investigate the following question:

Do homes built pre-1970 have higher average lead soil concentrations?

To perform this test, you’ll need the exide-property-covariates.csv file found in /courses/EDS222/data. This file contains the year each property was built.

Note

The question is about average lead soil concentrations. You’ll need to first get the average lead soil concentration for each property, then join your result to the property covariate data to get the year the house was built.

3. Simulate a negative result

Recall that a p-value is the probability of observing your sample if the null hypothesis is true. Therefore, if you draw a sample from two groups that part of the same population, you should get a p values less than 0.05 only 5% of the time. In other words, a negative result (no actual difference) will yield a significant p-value at the 0.05 threshold in 5% of samples. This is known as the false positive rate.

To simulate a negative result, perform the following.==

  1. Define a normally-distributed population. Pick any mean and standard deviation.
